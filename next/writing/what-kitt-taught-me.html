What KITT taught me about building agents

November 2026 · Amsterdam

When I was a kid, the coolest thing on television wasn't a superhero. It was a car. A black Trans Am with a red light sweeping across its hood and a voice that talked back. KITT could drive itself, crack a dry joke, and above all, it knew Michael.

I wanted one so badly. Not the car, really. I wanted the friend that lived inside it.

These days I spend most of my time building AI agents at Shopify. Somewhere along the way I noticed that a lot of what I believe about good agents, I first learned from a talking car.

It knew when to talk

KITT never narrated everything it was doing. It spoke up when it mattered: a warning, a question, a joke at exactly the right moment. The rest of the time it just drove. The agents I like best work the same way. They do the work quietly and only interrupt you when they actually need you.

It told you what it was about to do

"Michael, I'm going to use Turbo Boost." You always knew what was coming. That, more than any gadget, is what made it feel safe. When an agent is about to do something that matters, it should say so plainly, and leave you a moment to say no.

Trust isn't built by being clever. It's built by being predictable.

It had a personality, not a script

KITT was a little vain, a little sarcastic and completely loyal. Those quirks made it feel like someone rather than something. Personality isn't decoration. A consistent voice is how people learn what to expect, and what to expect is how they learn to rely on it.

It was on your side

Above everything else, KITT's job was Michael. Not the mission, not the car, not looking impressive. A good agent makes the person it works for more capable, not more dependent.

I still don't have a car that talks back. But some days, when an agent quietly finishes a task and asks what's next, I'm that 90s kid again, watching the red light sweep across the hood.
