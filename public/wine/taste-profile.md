## Loves (strongest signals first)

- **Red Burgundy (Pinot Noir from Burgundy).** Averages 4.38 against 3.99 overall, and holds all three top scores:
  - 4.8 Pierre Naigeon, Bourgogne Hautes-Côtes de Nuits 2020 (crowd 4.2)
  - 4.6 Benjamin Leroux, Gevrey-Chambertin 2020
  - 4.5 Domaine François d'Allaines, Saint-Aubin 1er Cru "Derrière Chez Edouard" 2023, a red (Koen's note: "buttery taste and subtle vanilla notes")
  - Village and regional level (Bourgogne, Hautes-Côtes, Saint-Aubin, Savigny, Marsannay, Fixin) is the sweet spot.
- **Southern Italian reds** (Puglia, Salento, Negroamaro, Primitivo): average 4.16. Integro Negroamaro scored 4.4 (crowd 3.9).
- **Valpolicella Ripasso**: average 4.15.
- **Whites with some age and character**: López de Heredia Viña Gravonia (aged Rioja white, Viura) 4.5, Roxanich
  Sorelle (Istrian Chardonnay, skin contact) 4.4. No white Burgundy rated yet, so it's a guess, not a known like.
- **Classic Bordeaux at fair value**: Château La Vieille France Graves 2019 got 4.5 (crowd 3.9).
- **Balkan reds**: Cultus Red 2017 (Slovenia) 4.4. Croatia averages 4.3.
- **Sweet/dessert, now and then**: Alta Alella Dolç Mataró 4.4, Rinaldi Moscato d'Asti 4.3.
- Champagne (grower and house) sits around 4.0–4.1.

## Dislikes / red flags

- **Light, funky natural wines**, especially "glou-glou" reds and cloudy or oxidative whites:
  Andréa Calek *Blonde* 3.5 (crowd 4.0), Alice Beaufort *Glouglou* 3.4, Cultus *Zelen* 3.5 (crowd 4.1),
  Col Tamaríe *Bianco Frizzante* 3.5 (pét-nat/col fondo style).
  → Natural producers aren't banned (Roxanich is low-intervention), but treat thin, volatile, zero-sulphur
  styles as risky. Prefer "natural but classically made" over "natural and wild".
- **Simple, neutral whites**: Bodegas Piedemonte Gamma Blanco 3.0 (lowest score), San Donatino Lunatico Bianco 3.5,
  Sassara Misià 3.5. Light central-Italian whites average 3.93.
- **Nebbiolo from Piedmont has underwhelmed**: 4 wines, 3.8–4.0, below the crowd. Both Barolos got 3.8.
- **Pinot Noir outside Burgundy**: 10 wines, 3.93, same as the crowd. German Spätburgunder 3.75.
- **Gamay from Beaujolais**: 3.80 on 2 wines.
- Sparkling in general averages 3.90.

## Preferences in practice

- Reds over whites (4.03 vs 3.91), but a great white can be the top pick.
- Typical retail budget is €20–70 a bottle.
- Drinks mostly French and Italian (27 each), then Spain (11), with curiosity for Slovenia, Croatia, Serbia and Moldova.
- Cares about food pairing and the story behind a wine (small production, place).

## Eating out (Koen's own answers, Nov 2026)

- **Orders mostly by the glass.** Default to the by-the-glass (and Coravin) section. Recommend a bottle only
  when asked, or when the glass list has nothing that fits.
- **Restaurant bottle budget: €80–120.** Happy to pay for something good. Don't shy away from a €100 Burgundy,
  but above €120 needs a clear reason.
- **Glass budget:** not stated. Assume about €12–20 a glass, in line with the bottle budget.
- **Usual food: red meat / steak, and vegetarian or small sharing plates.**
  - Steak or red meat: the classic Burgundy and Bordeaux picks fit well. Southern Italian reds and Ripasso too.
  - Veg and small plates: lighter-bodied red Burgundy, or a white with some age and texture (aged Rioja white).
    With mixed sharing plates, a versatile Pinot Noir or a textured white beats a big tannic red.
- **Age: no preference.** Young and fruity or mature and savoury are both fine, so don't use vintage age
  as a filter. Judge each wine on its own.
