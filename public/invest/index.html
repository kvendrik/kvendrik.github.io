    02 · The tool

    What an index fund is

    An index is a list: say, every listed company in the world, each counted by its size. An index fund simply buys everything on the list. An ETF is an index fund you buy and sell like a share.

    1. It owns everything

      One fund, thousands of companies. If a company grows, you own it. If one fails, it was a tiny slice. Nobody has to guess which is which.

    2. Nobody picks

      No star manager, no research team, no trading on hunches. The fund follows the list, so it costs very little to run: often 0.1% to 0.25% a year.

    3. You get the market

      You will never beat the market. You also won’t trail it by much, which is more than most professionals manage once their fees are counted.

    Picking stocks is a game played against professionals with more data, more time and faster computers. Before costs, the market’s return is by definition the average return of everyone in it. After costs, the average active investor has to do worse than the index, because they pay more. The numbers bear it out: the S&P SPIVA scorecards find that over 15 to 20 years roughly nine in ten actively managed US stock funds trail their index.

    There is a second reason, and it is stranger. Most stocks don’t do well. Hendrik Bessembinder looked at every US stock from 1926 to 2016 and found that just 4% of them created all of the stock market’s wealth above treasury bills. The other 96%, together, did no better than cash. If you pick a handful of stocks, you will probably miss the few that matter.

    Try it · 100 stocks, 4 of them winners

    Pick 10 and see if you find the needles.

    The 4 dark squares are the companies that will make the money. You just don’t know that yet.

    The index fund owns all 100, so it always owns all 4. A random 10 stocks misses every winner about 65% of the time.

    03 · Market cap weighted

    What you actually own

    A total world fund holds roughly 9,000 companies in about 50 countries. Each gets a share of your money in proportion to what it is worth on the stock market. Bigger companies get more.

    Why weigh by size

    The market does the work

    Prices already hold everyone’s best guess about every company. Weighing by size means you hold the market’s own view, and as prices move your weights move with them. There is nothing to rebalance and almost nothing to trade, which keeps costs down.

    Why the whole world

    Nobody knows the next winner

    In 1900 Britain was the world’s biggest stock market, about a quarter of it. The US was around 15%. Russia’s market was large too, and in 1917 it went to zero. Today the US is about two thirds. A world fund follows whoever leads without you having to call it.

    05 · The whole plan

    Five rules, then go live your life

    01

    Own the whole world.

    One total world index fund covers it. You don’t need a second one to “diversify” the first.

    02

    Keep costs close to zero.

    Fees are the one part of your return you control. Every tenth of a percent compounds against you for decades.

    03

    Don’t time it.

    Invest when you have money, not when it feels right. Nobody reliably knows when the next crash or the next rally is coming.

    04

    Match the risk to your stomach.

    Stocks can fall by half. If you would sell in a crash, hold some bonds so you won’t. The best plan is the one you can stick to.

    05

    Stay the course.

    Bogle’s phrase. The returns on the chart above went to the people who were still holding after the bad years, not to those who left.
